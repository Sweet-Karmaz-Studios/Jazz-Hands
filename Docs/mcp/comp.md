# Comp graphs

A comp graph is a compositing node graph inside one clip, as a timeline clip opens into Fusion in Resolve. `comp_create` gives a clip a graph with the clip's own picture coming in (`comp.in`, already placed by the clip's transform) wired to the output (`comp.out`), so nothing changes until nodes are added. What reaches the output is the clip's picture from there on: its effects after the graph, its opacity and blend still apply, and the timeline stays a layer stack around it.

`comp_node_add` adds a node by type: the graph's own `merge` (ports `background`, `foreground`, `mask`; `blend`, `opacity`, and the foreground's `position`, `scale`, `rotation`, `anchor`), `transform`, `matte` (ports `input` and `matte`; `mode` alpha, luma or inverted), `media` (another media item by id in its `media` parameter, at an `offset` in seconds, fitted), `plane3d`, `render3d` (ports `1` to `8`) and `out`; the 3D objects `text3d`, `shape3d`, `model3d`, `camera3d` and `light3d`; or any video effect or generator by its type (`video.blur.gaussian`, `video.key.chroma`, `gen.noise`). With `from` it is wired straight in. `comp_node_connect` wires a node into a port of another; a wire that would go round in a circle is refused (`would-cycle`), as is one from a node the graph does not have (`node-not-found`) and a second output (`output-exists`). Every node's parameters are set with `param_set` by the node's id, keyframed with `keyframe_add`, and its masks work as an effect's do.

A 3D render takes planes (a picture wired through `plane3d`, placed in depth and turned), text, shapes and models (each with its own parameters and a place: `position`, `z`, `rotation-x`, `rotation-y`, `rotation`, `scale`), a camera and lights, and draws them with the same renderer as the timeline's 3D layers. Without a camera it uses the default one.

Read a graph back with `comp_describe`: the nodes the output needs in the order they are drawn, what each reads through which port, its parameters, and the nodes nothing uses. Look at any node's picture with `render_frame` and its `node`.
