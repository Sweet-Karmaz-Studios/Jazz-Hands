# 3D layers, cameras and lights

Any picture clip on a video track can be made a 3D layer with `clip_set_3d`. It keeps its place, size and turn about Z (`transform.*`) and gains depth (`transform.z`, sequence pixels away from the viewer; negative comes towards the camera), a turn about X and Y (`transform.rotation-x` tips the top edge away, `transform.rotation-y` turns the right edge away) and a material (`material.ambient`, `material.diffuse`, `material.specular`, `material.roughness`). Set and animate them with `param_set` and `keyframe_add` like any other parameter. Made 3D, a clip does not move: at depth 0 with no turn the default camera sees it exactly where it was. Its effects and masks apply in its own space, before it is set in 3D, as in After Effects.

3D layers next to each other in the stack are drawn together as one scene, with a depth buffer, so layers that cross are cut where they meet. A flat clip, an adjustment layer or a transition between them starts a new scene. Cameras and lights do not break a run.

`camera_add` puts a `3d.camera` clip on the timeline; the topmost camera under the playhead is the one used, and without one the default camera sees the frame as it is. At rest it looks at the frame centre from in front. `dolly` moves it in with its point of interest, `orbit` and `tilt` swing it round the point of interest, `roll` turns it, a narrower `angle` zooms, and `dof` with `focus` and `aperture` blurs what is out of focus. Keyframe any of them on the camera clip for a move. Motion blur on the camera clip (`clip_set_motion_blur`) blurs the whole scene as the camera moves.

`light_add` puts a `3d.light` clip on: `point`, `spot`, `directional` or `ambient`. With no light a scene shows its layers unlit; once there is one, a layer is lit only by the lights there are, so add an ambient light for fill. A light with `shadows` throws shadows from every layer made to cast them (`clip_set_3d` with `castsShadows`). Check the result with `render_frame`: lighting and depth are easy to get wrong from numbers alone.

Geometry (Phase 48) lives in the same scenes. `text3d_add` puts extruded, bevelled text on: any installed font or one in the project's fonts folder, with `depth`, `bevel`, a face `color` and a `sideColor`, and `metallic` and `roughness`. `shape3d_add` puts a cube, sphere, cylinder, cone, torus or plane. `model3d_add` brings in a glTF 2.0 file (`.gltf` or `.glb`) with its own materials and textures, centred, sized by its largest side (`size`), its animation playing over the clip; `model3d_info` says first what is in a file, and what this reader leaves out (Draco compression, more than four bones a corner). Skinned characters and morph target animation play as the file has them. All three are always 3D, throw shadows, and move and turn with `transform.*` like a 3D layer; their looks are their own parameters, set with `param_set`.

An `environment` light (`light_add` with `kind` environment) surrounds the scene with a picture, an imported still given by its media id in its `image` parameter (equirectangular, HDR or EXR is best): it fills everything softly with its colours, and metal and polished surfaces reflect it, sharply when `roughness` is low.
